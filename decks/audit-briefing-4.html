Four checks that find most lead leaks

Loads. Dials. Form sends. No warnings.

Everything else is optimization. These four are survival.

The four checks

  • Loads — the main URL serves the real site, at a phone-sized viewport, with no error page
  • Dials — a tappable phone link outside decorative sliders, present on every key page
  • Form sends — a form exists wherever copy promises one, and a test entry actually arrives
  • No warnings — valid certificate, no browser alerts, from the customer's side

Evidence base: benchmark sheet (n=21).

Scoring

We score every audit with the published 25-point rubric: business basics /10, visibility & mobile gaps /10, fit & feasibility /5.

  • 20–25 — top priority
  • 14–19 — good
  • 8–13 — watchlist
  • below 8 — skip for now

A worked example (anonymized)

One electrical contractor's contact page instructs visitors to "use the form below." Source check: 0 form elements. No viewport meta. Zero tap-to-call links.

What did that cost them in lost jobs? We don't know — and we won't invent a number. What we do know: the fix is same-week mechanical work.

Method — how these numbers were produced

  • Rendered audit: every site loaded at a phone-sized viewport (390x844, Chromium), full-page capture, no cache
  • Source checks: form-element counts, tel: href inventory, viewport meta — cross-checked with an independent server-side fetch
  • Dating: every finding recorded with its inspection date; re-verified before publication
  • Scoring: published 25-point rubric (business basics /10, visibility and mobile gaps /10, fit and feasibility /5)
  • Anonymization: businesses appear as P01-P21; findings shared with each business privately first
  • Limitations: n=21, two regions; the study measures defect presence — it did not measure conversion rates or revenue impact

Findings vs recommendations: the counts in this deck are measured findings from the published audit. The fixes we recommend are our operating judgment, not study results.
