What Breaks on Contractor Websites

First rendered-audit results — October 2026, 21 local home-services sites, anonymized.

We loaded each site the way a customer does: a phone-sized screen, fresh browser, no cache. Every finding is dated and published.

Dataset: Local Home-Services Website Defect Benchmark (n=21)

What we found

  • 4 of 21 sites unreachable at their main web address
  • 5 of 21 serving invalid or mismatched TLS certificates
  • 8+ of 21 with contact-capture defects — promised forms that do not exist, no tappable phone number
  • 3 sites where conversion CTAs resolved to error pages
  • 2 sites still serving template demo copy
  • 4 sites with staleness signals (copyright 2011–2022)

Rendered audit, October 2026. All counts in the published benchmark sheet.

Anatomy of a working mobile site

  • A sticky tap-to-call header with a real tel: link — verified on iOS and Android viewports.
  • A form that actually sends — verified by submitting your own test entry, not by trusting the builder preview.
  • A valid certificate, checked from the customer's browser.

Three mechanical checks. We publish the snippets: open-source toolkit.

How sites decay silently

Migrations leave placeholder text live. Certificates expire on weekends. Plugin updates delete forms. None of it triggers an error the owner ever sees — the failures happen in other people's browsers.

Nobody reports a broken website. They just leave.

Method — how these numbers were produced

  • Rendered audit: every site loaded at a phone-sized viewport (390x844, Chromium), full-page capture, no cache
  • Source checks: form-element counts, tel: href inventory, viewport meta — cross-checked with an independent server-side fetch
  • Dating: every finding recorded with its inspection date; re-verified before publication
  • Scoring: published 25-point rubric (business basics /10, visibility and mobile gaps /10, fit and feasibility /5)
  • Anonymization: businesses appear as P01-P21; findings shared with each business privately first
  • Limitations: n=21, two regions; the study measures defect presence — it did not measure conversion rates or revenue impact

Findings vs recommendations: the counts in this deck are measured findings from the published audit. The fixes we recommend are our operating judgment, not study results.
